Most martial artists in the world are completely out of their minds, spending decades meditating in freezing caves and swinging swords like crazy monkeys just to prove they are slightly better at violence than the monkey standing next to them. But in a realm packed with certified lunatics, our boy Yi Zaha proudly stands as the undisputed CEO of absolute madness. Known across the entire continent as the Crazy Demon, Zaha never cared about noble clans, prestigious martial alliances, or demonic cults; if someone acted like a corrupt scumbag, he simply showed up, broke their balance, and taught them a lesson in humility. That brings us to the windy peak of the Ten Thousand Mile Cliff, where our boy is battered, bleeding, and completely surrounded by an army of elite Demon Cult enforcers after pulling off the most absurd heist in history. He just broke into the Demon Cult's inner vault and stole their most sacred treasure, the legendary Heavenly Pearl. For three days and three nights, the cult's top executioner, the Left Hand of Light, has been hunting him across mountains without giving him a single second of rest. Most people trapped against a bottomless gorge would panic, cry, or beg for their lives, but Zaha looks at the handsome assassin and immediately begins roasting his terrible life choices. Dangling the glowing celestial orb over the thousand-foot drop like a piece of cheap fruit, Zaha casually warns everyone to keep their distance, proudly bragging that he is a master of deception who never falls for honey traps. The cult master deadpans right back, emotionally damaging our boy by pointing out that he never fell for honey traps because women simply found him too terrifying to approach, forcing the cult to hire an everyday villager just to get his attention. Deeply offended by the roast, Zaha complains that three days of running meant zero food, zero sleep, and worst of all, zero bathroom breaks. When the cult master demands the sacred treasure back, Zaha drops all negotiations, pops the ancient divine relic into his mouth like a throat lozenge, and happily announces that it tastes like fresh peaches. The cult commander completely loses his temper, ordering his entire squad to charge in a desperate frenzy. But even running on empty, our boy demonstrates pure black-air-force energy. Slipping beneath flashing blades with supernatural footwork, Zaha unleashes thunderous palm strikes that shatter iron armor and launch cult soldiers tumbling across the rocky bluffs. When the Left Hand lunges forward with crackling electric qi, the violent collision detonates the cliff edge, hurling Zaha straight backward into the misty void. Falling toward certain doom, Zaha bursts out laughing, gives the entire cult a cheeky wave goodbye, and leaves them with the ultimate headache. But instead of turning into a red smudge at the bottom of the canyon, Zaha’s soul enters a glowing celestial realm inside the ancient relic. A towering divine spirit appears before him, demanding to know why a madman stole the sacred pearl. Looking the deity dead in the eye, Zaha speaks with complete honesty: he took it because corrupt cult tyrants view regular people as disposable insects, and he refused to let them become invincible gods. Pleased by his chaotic yet righteous heart, the ancient spirit grants him a miraculous final lifeline. A blinding light envelopes his vision, and when Zaha finally opens his eyes, the smell of burning blood is replaced by morning rain and cheap tavern wine. Looking down at his hands, his battle-hardened scars have vanished, replaced by the smooth, skinny fingers of a broke twenty-year-old waiter. He just regressed ten years into the past, waking up inside the humble inn where his journey first began. The local thugs think he is just an easy target who can be bullied for loose change, completely unaware that a reincarnated demon just clocked in for his morning shift.
