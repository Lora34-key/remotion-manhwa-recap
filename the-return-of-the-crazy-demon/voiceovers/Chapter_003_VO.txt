Kicking open the grand double doors of the Plum Blossom Brothel, our boy Yi Zaha strolls straight into the crowded establishment with the swagger of an emperor inspecting his royal estate. The entire ground floor freezes in stunned silence as gamblers, hostesses, and armed bouncers turn to stare at the scrawny kid who was supposed to be weeping in bed with broken ribs. Whispers immediately spread like wildfire across the gambling tables, with thugs laughing that the local errand boy must have suffered severe brain damage from his recent beating to willingly march right back into the lion’s den. In their eyes, an errand boy is the absolute lowest organism in the martial food chain, someone who exists purely to mop floors and take punches without complaining. Two heavily muscled bouncers step forward, cracking their knuckles and demanding to know why a complete nobody has the nerve to crash their private establishment. Zaha merely gives them a deadpan stare, pointing out that this is an entertainment house and asking if they are in the habit of interrogating paying customers who simply want a cold drink. Before the goons can drag him into the alleyway, a smooth, condescending voice echoes down from the second-floor balcony. Leaning over the polished banister is Cha Sung-tae, the brothel’s silver-haired manager, sporting a sharp red silk robe and an amused smirk. Sung-tae waves his hand, casually ordering his men to stand down because turning away a paying customer is bad for business, entirely unaware that he just invited an unstoppable apex predator into his dining room. Led into a lavish private booth, Zaha sits down cross-legged, pours himself a cup of house liquor, and immediately grimaces in disgust, realizing they are serving overpriced, watered-down garbage to their guests. Before he can complain about the terrible beverage quality, the private door slams open, revealing Dongguk, the massive thug captain who personally led the mob that jumped Zaha a few days ago. Towering over the table with veins popping on his forehead, Dongguk starts screaming obscenities, asking how an insignificant tavern errand boy has the audacity to order drinks under their roof. While most normal people would be trembling in terror, Zaha remains completely unfazed, calmly sipping his tea and casually pouring a cup for the raging giant. He politely asks Dongguk to take a seat, acting like a friendly uncle trying to have a civilized chat with an unruly toddler. Confused by our boy’s absolute lack of survival instinct, Dongguk sits down across from him, sneering that getting his head bashed in must have knocked Zaha's common sense loose. To make the scene even wilder, Zaha specifically requests that courtesan Chae Hyang be brought in to pour their drinks, proving once and for all that he doesn’t hold a grudge over the fabricated rumors. Dongguk’s crew gathers around the private chamber, bursting into loud, mocking laughter as they marvel at the errand boy's suicidal confidence. Outside the door, Chae Hyang clutches her silk dress in nervous dread, convinced that the poor boy is about to be buried under the floorboards. Dongguk smirks with cruel satisfaction, admitting that if he had known the boy had this much spine, he would have beaten him twice as hard during their last encounter. Telling the hostesses to step outside, the gang captain leans across the table, cracking his heavy fists, and tells Zaha to finish his final drink before they drag him out back to properly finish the job. Zaha calmly sets down his porcelain cup, offers them one last polite smile, and prepares to teach these overconfident mobsters why you should never provoke a retired demon.
