The moment the sliding paper doors close, the private dining chamber erupts into violent, one-sided chaos that rattles the entire brothel foundation. Out in the polished hallway, courtesan Chae Hyang trembles with wide eyes as deafening thuds, cracking wood, and muffled screams echo through the wooden walls. She expects the arrogant thugs to drag out a mangled errand boy, but the door suddenly bursts open, revealing a spectacle that defies every rule of the underworld. Walking through the splintered frame with dead-eyed composure, our boy Yi Zaha drags Dongguk by his silk collar like a sack of dirty laundry, casually tossing the two-hundred-pound squad leader onto a groaning pile of his own battered goons. Within two minutes of stepping into the back room, our boy dismantled six seasoned mobsters with bare fists, dishing out severe emotional and physical damage without mussing a single strand of hair. Every single bully who laughed at the defenseless errand boy days ago is now stacked like cordwood across the floorboards, clutching shattered jaws, fractured ribs, and praying for their lives. Taking a seat directly on top of the groaning pile of gangsters like it’s a luxury leather couch, Zaha casually crosses his legs and calls out to the terrified madam standing in the corridor. He rudely addresses her as an old hag and demands a fresh bottle of Dukuang liquor, pointing out that their previous drinks tasted like watered-down gutter juice. Trembling in her silk robes, the madam frantically stammers that their wine is top-shelf, but Zaha merely glares at her with icy malice, calmly reminding her that while he makes it a strict personal rule never to strike women who don't practice martial arts, she had better watch her tone before she finds out what happens when his patience runs out. Looking down at Dongguk, who is gasping for breath beneath his boots, Zaha gently pats the gang captain on the head and tells him to start laughing, asking why the comedians who found him so hilarious five minutes ago are suddenly so quiet. Before the panic can spread through the rest of the building, brothel manager Cha Sung-tae rushes into the room, only to freeze dead in his tracks. Cold sweat pours down the silver-haired manager’s neck as his mind fails to process the terrifying reality before him: the scrawny orphan who sweeps the inn floors just single-handedly folded his entire security detail like lawn chairs. Sung-tae always prided himself on being a cunning underworld mastermind who could outsmart anyone in the prefecture, but staring into Zaha's unblinking, psychotic gaze, he realizes his life is hanging by a razor-thin thread. He immediately switches into full survival mode, plastering a polite, professional customer service smile across his face. He personally brings out an authentic, unopened ceramic jug of vintage Dukuang wine, bowing low as he pours our boy a fresh cup. Zaha takes a slow sip, nodding in approval before calling out the brothel's scam, noting that they only serve real quality once you beat their staff half to death. When Sung-tae tries to speak, Zaha cuts him off mid-sentence, coldly ordering him to stop speaking so casually and warning him that his sly, narrow eyes are making our boy feel personally disrespected. Recognizing the predatory aura of an absolute lunatic who has killed thousands, Sung-tae swallows his pride, bows his head in deep submission, and promises to speak with absolute reverence. Zaha studies the trembling manager with a terrifying smile, giving him high marks for his polite manners, fully aware that he just acquired his very first lackey. And with the brothel's security thoroughly crushed, the real restructuring of the town's criminal underworld is officially about to begin.
