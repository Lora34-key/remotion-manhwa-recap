Enjoying a warm bowl of beef broth while sitting across from his newly reformed lackey, our boy Yi Zaha listens intently as Cha Sung-tae spills every single secret about the local criminal hierarchy. Sung-tae nervously explains that the brothel's true owner isn’t just some everyday pimp; he is currently meeting with high-ranking envoys from the infamous Black Cat Union, a brutal criminal syndicate that controls several surrounding territories. The plan is to officially merge forces and establish an untouchable underworld empire right here in Ilyang Prefecture, meaning anyone who steps out of line will be swiftly erased from existence. Instead of trembling in fear like any sensible person would, Zaha calmly takes another sip of his soup, wipes his mouth with a napkin, and casually drops an absolute bombshell: he decides right then and there that he is going to create his own martial sect, and he expects Sung-tae, the brothel owner, and every single thug in town to sign up as his obedient, low-wage employees. Sung-tae stares at him like he just grew a second head, stammering that hardened gang bosses with dozens of armed killers would rather swallow burning coals than take orders from a lowly tavern errand boy. Zaha merely smiles with dead-eyed confidence, replying that whether they are willing or not doesn’t matter, because he has already made up his mind, and arguing with him is historically terrible for one's long-term life expectancy. Realizing that running a brand-new martial sect requires proper lethal gear rather than wooden broomsticks, Zaha strolls down to the local Dragon Head Smithy, a legendary underground workshop run by a burly, red-haired blacksmith known for forging the sharpest steel in the province. Walking through the blinding sparks and clanging hammers, our boy is surrounded by hulking ironworkers who scoff at the skinny kid browsing for weapons. But when the master smith steps forward to test the young stranger's resolve, Zaha doesn’t flinch for a microsecond. Inspecting the heavy iron blades with the seasoned eye of a martial grandmaster who has broken countless sacred weapons across hundreds of bloody battlefields, our boy speaks with the cold authority of an ancient warlord, making it crystal clear that he isn’t here to play petty street games; he is here to cleanse this rotten prefecture of trash and establish absolute order. Completely captivated by the boy’s terrifying intensity and unshakeable aura, the master blacksmith lets out a hearty laugh, hands over an entire crate of premium daggers and sabers completely free of charge, and tells our boy they can finish their business if he manages to survive the night. Slipping the cold steel beneath his robes, Zaha strolls back to the inn under the cover of midnight, ready for a well-deserved nap. But as he rounds the street corner, he finds a bizarre spectacle waiting in the courtyard: several armed mobsters are sweating under the moonlight, furiously digging a massive six-foot hole directly in front of the inn's main entrance. Sitting comfortably on a wooden bench while sharpening a jagged hunting dagger is Cho Gwan, the notorious second brother of the Cho crime family. Looking up with a sadistic grin, the gang enforcer casually greets our boy, gleefully announcing that they were planning to drag Zaha outside the moment they finished digging his fresh grave. In their minds, burying an orphan errand boy is just an easy weekend chore that requires zero effort, completely oblivious to the fact that they just spent three exhausting hours digging their own burial plot. And as Zaha slowly loosens his shoulders with a dangerous grin, the quiet night is about to erupt into pure, unadulterated violence.
