Sitting across a banquet table loaded with braised pork, steaming dumplings, and fried chicken, our boy Yi Zaha treats Cha Sung-tae to a dinner that feels less like a celebration and more like a calculated psychological interrogation. Looking at the trembling brothel manager, Zaha politely urges him to eat his fill, casually pointing out that every single dish was paid for with blood money earned by extorting young women and selling watered-down gutter wine to unsuspecting patrons. Watching Sung-tae choke on his food in humiliated silence gives us a fascinating look into our boy’s unorthodox leadership philosophy. Zaha doesn't just want mindless muscle; he forces his subordinates to confront the ugly reality of their past crimes, breaking down their petty criminal egos until they realize that their entire life was built on cheap scams. When Sung-tae snaps under the pressure and threatens to quit the underworld entirely to hide in the mountains, Zaha doesn't raise his voice or draw a blade. With masterclass manipulation, our boy casually mentions that once they conquer the district, he plans to expand into three separate luxury establishments, but since Sung-tae wants to retire like a coward, he will simply have to find a smarter manager. The second real power and wealth are dangled before him, Sung-tae immediately abandons his dreams of mountain hermit life, proudly declaring that he is ready to serve for the long haul. But before the manager can finish his drink, Zaha stands up and casually begins stripping off his outer garments right in the middle of the dining hall, bluntly ordering Sung-tae to hand over his expensive blue silk robes. When the baffled manager asks why an errand boy needs high-end merchant attire, Zaha doesn’t waste time explaining; in the martial underworld, appearance dictates perception, and walking into an enemy stronghold looking like an impoverished servant immediately puts you at a psychological disadvantage. Dressed in sharp, flowing silk with a forged blade strapped to his sash, our boy strolls straight into the private manor of Cho Il-gwon, the ruthless eldest brother of the Cho crime syndicate. Slipping past the outer guards without raising a single alarm, Zaha walks into the grand council chamber and takes a seat directly on the massive master throne at the head of the room, casually resting his cheek against his fist. This is an elite tactical power move: by claiming the high seat before the owner even arrives, Zaha instantly forces the gang leader to look up at him, stripping away his home-turf advantage before a single word is spoken. When Cho Il-gwon finally enters, the gang leader freezes in sheer disbelief. Seeing an uninvited guest sitting comfortably in his personal seat of power with legs crossed and zero intention of standing up completely shatters his mental equilibrium. Assuming this mysterious, sharp-dressed young man must be a high-ranking executive from the regional Black Cat Union, Cho Il-gwon tries to maintain diplomatic respect, asking what executive rank the visitor holds within the syndicate. Zaha slowly looks up, an amused smirk playing across his lips as he casually asks if the boss genuinely fails to recognize the person who serves him tea every morning. Leaning closer under the lantern light, Cho Il-gwon’s eyes widen in absolute horror as the terrifying realization hits him like a freight train: the man sitting on his throne isn't an elite mob boss, but the quiet, discarded errand boy from the local tavern. Calling our boy completely insane, Cho Il-gwon reaches for his broadsword, completely blind to the fact that he just stepped into a trap where his fate has already been sealed.
