Soaking in a steaming wooden tub filled with fragrant hot water, our boy Yi Zaha closes his eyes, letting out a satisfied sigh as the lingering stench of his marrow cleansing finally washes away. Cha Sung-tae had literally drawn a knife earlier, threatening our boy from across the room and screaming that no civilized human being should ever smell like an open sewer, forcing Zaha to take over the brothel's premier bath suite. To make the scene even more absurd, courtesan Chae Hyang enters to gently scrub the water across his shoulders, creating an atmosphere that would make any young man blush. But watching Zaha's internal thoughts gives us a hilarious, deeply relatable glimpse into his fractured psychology. Even surrounded by warm water and a beautiful woman, our boy cannot stop dwelling on the memory of the Demon Cult executioner roasting his terrible dating history on the cliff edge. He realizes with bitter irritation that the assassin was completely right: throughout his entire past life as the feared Crazy Demon, women never fell for honey traps around him because he gave off the intense, terrifying aura of an unhinged homicidal maniac. Just as he tries to banish the insult from his mind, the bathhouse doors slam open with violent urgency. Cha Sung-tae bursts into the steam, his silver hair disheveled and his face pale with absolute panic as he screams that a massive blaze has broken out. When Zaha lazily asks why the manager is losing his mind over a little smoke, Sung-tae drops the punchline: the fire didn’t start anywhere in the entertainment district; someone just torched Zaha’s private quarters at the inn. This tactical escalation highlights the classic cowardice of the criminal underworld. Whenever mob enforcers realize they cannot defeat an opponent in a direct martial confrontation, they instinctively resort to arson, hoping to burn the threat alive while they sleep rather than risk a blade to the throat. Stepping outside in fresh robes with damp hair, Zaha arrives at the smoking ruins of his courtyard, only to find an arrogant swordsman in vibrant yellow silk waiting with a drawn broadsword. Puffed up with classic Murim arrogance, the yellow-robed mercenary commands our boy to draw his weapon for an honorable duel. Zaha looks him up and down with clinical boredom, calmly stating that using a forged steel blade against someone of his mediocre caliber would be an insult to the art of swordsmanship. Enraged by the blatant disrespect, the mercenary lunges forward with a high-velocity downward slash designed to cleave our boy in half. But in the eyes of a reincarnated grandmaster, the strike is hopelessly telegraphed. Slipping inside the blade’s arc by a fraction of an inch, Zaha plants an explosive open-palm counter-strike directly into the swordsman's solar plexus, the kinetic shockwave blasting the grown man twenty feet through the air before crashing him onto the scorched dirt. As the mercenary writhes in the mud, gasping for air with shattered ribs, Zaha looks down at him in mild disgust, telling him to stop overreacting because that strike didn't even use a fraction of his genuine strength. In a world full of theatrical warriors obsessed with flashy poses, our boy continues to prove that raw kinetic efficiency will always fold arrogance into the dirt. Kneeling over the groaning mercenary, Zaha inspects the bronze medallion hanging from his belt, realizing that this arson attack wasn't a random hit; the regional Black Rabbit Union has officially mobilized their external enforcers. And looking toward the northern road where the smoke rises into the night sky, our boy prepares to take this fight straight to the syndicate's front doorstep.
