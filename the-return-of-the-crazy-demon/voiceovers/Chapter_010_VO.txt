Flames roar against the night sky as the Zaha Inn burns into a pile of smoldering embers, but the real spectacle isn't the fire; it's the total collapse of the town's criminal hierarchy. Standing in the middle of the blazing courtyard, our boy Yi Zaha looks down at the defeated yellow-robed swordsman, who turns out to be Cho Sam-pyung, the youngest brother of the notorious Cho family. In less than twenty-four hours, our boy systematically wiped out all three ruling mob brothers, leaving the local gangsters wandering the streets like headless chickens. Cha Sung-tae watches from the edge of the crowd with chills running down his spine, finally realizing that the scrawny kid who used to sweep tavern floors has completely conquered the prefecture's underworld without breaking a single drop of sweat. Most ordinary martial masters would be weeping over the loss of their property, but our boy brilliantly turns a disaster into an unprecedented economic upgrade: since his old wooden shack is gone, he simply confiscates the finest luxury suites in the entire entertainment district without paying a single copper. Standing amidst the smoke, Zaha officially announces the birth of a new organization: the Low-Down Sect, or Hao Mun. In the wider lore of Murim, high-and-mighty sects sneer at the Low-Down Sect as a pathetic collective of beggars, courtesans, cooks, and street urchins. But from a strategic and historical standpoint, Zaha knows that common workers are the true bloodstream of society; they hear every secret, see every transaction, and control the entire flow of information across the continent. When Sung-tae timidly asks whether he should start addressing Zaha as the Sect Leader, our boy simply tells him to do whatever he wants. But then Sung-tae asks a deeply vulnerable, serious question: why did Zaha spare his life when he ruthlessly executed the Cho brothers? Instead of giving a dramatic speech about redemption or friendship, Zaha deadpans right back that he kept the manager alive purely so he would have someone entertaining to mess with. On the surface, it sounds like an unhinged joke, but psychologically, it is pure tactical genius: by keeping his subordinates permanently off-balance and guessing his true intentions, Zaha ensures that nobody ever dares to plot a betrayal. Moving his operations into the lavish upper suites of the brothel, our boy convenes a high-stakes council with the two remaining merchant guild elders, a pair of greedy, corrupt officials who previously funded the Cho brothers' illegal rackets. Sitting comfortably across from the sweating bureaucrats, Zaha delivers a masterclass in aggressive deadline negotiation. He places his hand on his forged steel blade and casually informs them that they have until the hostess returns with their wine to decide whether they will surrender their commercial assets and cooperate with his new sect, or fight him to the death right now. Corrupt politicians love to stall and make empty promises, but by placing a literal five-minute timer on their mortality, Zaha shatters their defense mechanisms. The moment Miss Sun’s footsteps echo outside the door, the terrified elders completely fold, breaking into nervous sweats, plastering fake smiles across their faces, and desperately swearing that they will personally smooth over all relations with the regional Black Cat Union. Smiling back with cold, predatory amusement, Zaha accepts their total surrender, knowing that the foundation of his underworld empire is officially locked into place. But deep within the surrounding mountains, the true leaders of the regional syndicate are already receiving word that their provincial branch has gone completely silent, setting up a massive collision course with a ruthless enemy that operates on a continental scale. And as the morning sun rises over the city, the newly christened Sect Leader is more than ready for war.
