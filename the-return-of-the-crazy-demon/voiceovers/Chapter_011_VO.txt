Watching the two corrupt merchant guild elders bow their heads in terrified submission, Cha Sung-tae cannot help but ask why our boy Yi Zaha let them walk out alive instead of executing them on the spot like the Cho brothers. This moment highlights the sharp strategic intellect operating beneath Zaha’s wild exterior. Killing low-level enforcers is one thing, but butchering prominent commercial leaders creates an administrative vacuum that forces regional superpowers, specifically the Black Mystic Union, to intervene directly. In our boy’s tactical assessment, the regional syndicate doesn’t care who collects the protection money in Ilyang Prefecture as long as the quotas are paid on time, and drawing the personal attention of the Slaughterer, the terrifying grandmaster of the Twelve Heavenly Palms, would be suicidal while his youthful dantian is still consolidating. To keep the town running smoothly, Zaha promotes Sung-tae to acting administrator of the newly christened Hao Clan, tasking him with auditing the brothel's accounts and seizing all hidden syndicate assets. But our boy couples this corporate promotion with an unmistakable death threat, casually warning Sung-tae that if a single silver coin goes missing from the books, the punishment won't be a demotion, but an immediate grave beside Cho Gwan. Realizing that his new CEO considers capital punishment standard company policy, Sung-tae scurries off to manage the books with trembling diligence, fully aware that stealing even pocket change from a reincarnated madman is the fastest way to get erased from the mortal realm. Later that afternoon, a cold, torrential downpour engulfs the prefecture, turning the dirt streets into treacherous mud. Stepping outside the tea house with his forged blade strapped to his back, Zaha spots a sinister figure emerging through the grey mist, wearing a drenched straw conical hat and black assassin robes. The stranger identifies himself as Wi Seon-woo of the Black Reconnaissance, demanding to know why a tavern errand boy had the audacity to execute their undercover operative, Neung Ji-seok. Looking into the assassin’s shadowed face, Zaha immediately recognizes him: in his past life, the infamous Sword Demon Twins were notorious underworld hitmen who hunted our boy across the northern provinces before he turned them into fertilizer. Seeing that Wi Seon-woo is several years younger and hasn't yet reached his peak master rank, Zaha meets the threat with pure comedic disdain. When the assassin declares that our boy will die in the mud for burning down the syndicate's local investments, Zaha doesn't flinch. Instead, he looks up at the man's dripping straw hat, completely ignores the death threat, and starts mercilessly roasting the assassin's receding hairline, pointing out that his plans are going to fail just as miserably as his bald scalp failed to hold onto hair. Psychologically, this is classic Crazy Demon warfare: while ordinary swordsmen spend five minutes trying to look mysterious and intimidating in the rain, Zaha instantly shatters their edgy, brooding persona with personal emotional damage. This unexpected confrontation marks the first external test of the fledgling Hao Clan, proving that Zaha's aggressive local takeover has officially sent shockwaves beyond the prefecture's borders. Enraged beyond reason by the bald joke, Wi Seon-woo draws his jagged broadsword, the rain hissing against cold steel as the dark alley transforms into a lethal execution ground. But facing an assassin he has already butchered once before in another lifetime, our boy casually rests his hand on his scabbard, completely unfazed by the storm, standing ready to prove that a seasoned apex predator never loses his edge, regardless of what century, timeline, or weather conditions he finds himself fighting in. And with steel flashing through the storm, the rain-soaked duel begins.
