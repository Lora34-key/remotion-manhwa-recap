Torrential rain thunders against the cobblestone street as the life-and-death standoff begins, but before Wi Seon-woo can swing his jagged broadsword, the tense atmosphere is completely ruined by pure slapstick comedy. Cha Sung-tae, desperately trying to prove his loyalty as the newly appointed administrator of the Hao Clan, attempts to sneak up behind the assassins through the pouring rain, only to slip on the wet mud and face-plant directly into a brown puddle. Standing up with his expensive silk robes soaked in grime, the brothel manager completely forgets about the armed assassins and starts furiously whining about ruining his brand-new outfit. Watching his right-hand man bungle a stealth assassination in broad daylight, Zaha looks over with deadpan disbelief, asking if Sung-tae genuinely believed that noisy, clumsy maneuver would ever work on seasoned killers. Sung-tae stubbornly argues that if the weather had been dry, he would have decapitated both assassins cleanly, treating an elite underworld ambush like a minor inconvenience. Standing ten feet away with their weapons drawn, Wi Seon-woo and his twin brother tremble with volcanic rage, their minds short-circuiting as they realize their targets are casually bickering about laundry in the middle of a deadly confrontation. Taking the psychological disrespect to a whole new level, Zaha gazes directly at Wi Seon-woo’s shivering, rain-soaked scalp, hilariously remarking that because the assassin lacks a single strand of hair, the heavy raindrops look like tiny dancers performing a choreography across his smooth dome. That brutal roast snaps Wi Seon-woo’s final thread of sanity; screaming in blind fury, both Sword Demon Twins lunge forward in a coordinated pincer attack, their heavy blades whistling through the downpour with lethal intent. In the eyes of ordinary martial artists, the Sword Demon Twins are terrifying killers whose synchronized strikes are mathematically impossible to evade. But to our boy Yi Zaha, who mastered the spatial geometry of combat across a thousand bloody battlefields, their trajectories are as obvious as daylight. By circulating the refined silver qi through his arm meridians, Zaha condenses his internal force into the razor-sharp edge of his forged blade. Shifting his weight by a fraction of an inch, Zaha lets the incoming steel slice through empty raindrops before executing a single, blinding horizontal draw that unleashes an arc of crimson sword qi. The compressed kinetic wave cuts through the storm, severing their weapons and cleanly cutting down both elite assassins before their boots even hit the ground. The heavy rain instantly washes the crimson blood down the stone gutters, leaving our boy standing immaculate and untouched amidst the downpour. Seeing the two legendary hitmen lying lifeless in the mud, Cha Sung-tae’s humorous bravado completely evaporates, replaced by absolute, petrified reverence. This is the exact moment Sung-tae's mindset permanently shifts: he stops viewing our boy as a lucky, unhinged kid and finally recognizes that he is serving an immortal warlord who operates on a plane of existence mortal men cannot comprehend. Falling to his knees in the soaking rain, the manager bows with trembling hands, stammering out his deepest gratitude for Zaha's hard work. Looking down at his shaking underling with cold detachment, Zaha wipes the rainwater from his blade, casually telling the manager to stop acting so unnaturally polite and return to his usual insolent self. In Zaha’s world, true power doesn't need groveling sycophants; it demands competent results, and with the Black Reconnaissance's elite vanguard officially neutralized, the rest of the syndicate's local foot soldiers are about to learn what happens when they surround a reincarnated demon in the dead of night, setting up an immediate clash that will cleanse the streets of Ilyang once and for all.
