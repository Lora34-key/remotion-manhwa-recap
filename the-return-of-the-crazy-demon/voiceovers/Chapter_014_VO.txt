Standing along the bustling thoroughfare of Ilyang Prefecture, our boy Yi Zaha is approached by an elite emissary from the Golden Phoenix Pavilion, an influential stronghold operating directly under the regional Black Mystic Union. Dressed in polished dark silk with an arrogant smirk plastered across his face, the envoy delivers an absolute ultimatum on behalf of his master: if our boy wishes to continue operating as the local underworld ruler, he must immediately travel to their mountain estate, prostrate himself before the Pavilion Master, and pledge total fealty to the union. The envoy condescendingly explains that complying will guarantee his survival and prevent innocent townspeople from being caught in a bloodbath, but if he refuses, the only option left is to flee the prefecture forever like a whipped dog. This moment showcases the brilliant psychological contrast between standard martial artists and the Crazy Demon. Any hot-headed young protagonist would draw his blade and slaughter the messenger on the spot, but Zaha operates on a completely different level of strategic calculation. He understands that regional syndicates operate entirely on bureaucratic extortion; they assume that an errand boy who eliminated the Cho brothers is merely an overconfident street brawler who can be easily intimidated by formal martial authority. Looking the envoy in the eye with mild amusement, Zaha calmly replies that running away or groveling would permanently strip him of his manhood, meaning he has no choice but to pay their master a personal visit. Furthermore, our boy dryly remarks that it would be terribly impolite to greet such an influential figure empty-handed or wearing ordinary tavern work clothes. Rather than letting the envoy escort him like a captive, Zaha tells the messenger to head back first and prepare the tea, promising to arrive under his own power. Returning to the brothel suites, our boy dresses in bespoke crimson and white robes, straps his forged steel blade to his sash, and strolls out of the city gates beneath the glowing orange sunset. Walking through the winding mountain roads alone, Zaha's internal thoughts reveal the deep historical philosophy that guides his actions. In the chaotic landscape of Kangho, regional syndicates maintain control through a manufactured illusion of absolute dominance, relying on lavish mountain compounds and armed garrisons to terrify local leaders into submission before a single punch is thrown. But to a reincarnated grandmaster who spent his past life toppling imperial sects, a fortified mountain manor is nothing more than a convenient slaughterhouse with closed doors. Arriving before the massive iron gates of the Golden Phoenix Pavilion, our boy calmly announces his presence, stepping through the portal with the relaxed posture of a noble lord inspecting a vacation villa. The courtyard is lined with dozens of elite martial artists brandishing drawn spears and curved scimitars, forming a suffocating gauntlet of lethal intimidation designed to break a man's courage. Standing atop the stone pavilion is the Pavilion Master himself, a colossal, broad-shouldered titan draped in heavy bear pelts and brandishing a massive iron executioner's blade. Sizing up the slender young man in flowing crimson silk, the titan sneers in disbelief, unable to reconcile the legendary rumors of a ruthless underworld conqueror with the boyish face of an inn errand boy. But staring back through the ranks of armed killers, Zaha offers a thin, predatory smile, ready to teach this provincial warlord why you should never summon an unhinged demon to your dinner table, especially when you are completely trapped inside your own locked courtyard. And as the heavy iron gates slam shut with an ominous echo behind him, the stage is set for an absolute, merciless bloodbath.
