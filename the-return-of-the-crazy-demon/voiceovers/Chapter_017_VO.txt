Deep within the subterranean grand hall of the Black Mystic Union, an oppressive, suffocating silence smothers the cavernous chamber as dozens of elite martial commanders sit frozen in their high-backed chairs. Looming atop the central dais on an obsidian throne is the Union Master himself, his identity concealed behind a menacing black-and-gold rabbit mask that glints malevolently under the flickering torchlight. Kneeling at the foot of the throne is a blood-soaked intelligence scout, trembling so violently his forehead repeatedly bangs against the polished stone floor. When the Union Master coldly demands an explanation for the complete radio silence from the Golden Phoenix Pavilion, the scout stammers out a report so absurd that several commanders stifle disbelieving chuckles: the pavilion was completely raided, its wealth embezzled, and the colossal, bear-pelt Pavilion Master was beaten to death by a twenty-year-old tavern errand boy named Yi Zaha. Watching the terrifying Union Master process this information is a masterclass in psychological comedy. For several long seconds, the supreme warlord merely blinks behind his mask, his internal logic breaking down as he repeats the scout's words aloud, demanding to know if an executive of a multi-province criminal syndicate was genuinely slaughtered by a kid whose primary job description is sweeping tavern floors and carrying noodle bowls. The scout tearfully confirms that the errand boy not only dropped the Pavilion Master in three seconds flat, but also captured their top inspector, Sima Bi, and established an unauthorized splinter faction called the Matchless Ignoble Clan. This moment brilliantly exposes the profound vulnerability of massive underworld syndicates: they spend decades building terrifying reputations through bureaucratic terror, yet when confronted with a chaotic anomaly who completely ignores their hierarchy, their entire system paralyzes in bewilderment. Realizing that allowing an errand boy to casually annex syndicate territory would destroy their authority across Kangho, the Union Master drops his calm facade, his killing intent flooding the chamber with glacial pressure. He officially mobilizes the Black Reconnaissance's apex enforcers, the legendary Twin Ghosts of the Ringed Blade, ordering them to descend upon Ilyang Prefecture and erase every living soul connected to the Zaha Inn. Meanwhile, hundreds of miles to the north, our boy Yi Zaha is completely unconcerned by the impending storm. Sitting on the freshly laid wooden beams of the reconstructed inn, Zaha coordinates with Sima Bi to install subterranean defensive tunnels and fortified escape routes, treating the upcoming syndicate invasion like an anticipated routine delivery. To ensure his inner circle can survive, Zaha subjects Cha Sung-tae to an agonizing executive martial boot camp, forcing the silver-haired manager to sprint laps across the courtyard while balancing boiling teacups on his head. Sung-tae weeps into the sawdust, begging for mercy, but Zaha merely points out that surviving an assassin's strike requires disciplined legwork, not smooth excuses. But our boy's most terrifying preparation happens behind closed doors, where he begins experimenting with medicinal herbs and potent digestive roots gathered from local apothecaries. In Zaha’s unhinged tactical philosophy, why waste precious internal qi clashing blades against dozens of armored henchmen when a single teaspoon of powdered root slipped into their water supply can incapacitate an entire army in the nearest outhouse? With the Twin Ghosts already riding hard through the mountain passes and a legion of ruthless shadow operatives descending on the prefecture, our boy smiles into the darkness, completely ready to prove that the wildest predator in the province doesn't hide behind an expensive gold mask—he serves noodles by day, brews biological warfare by night, and rewrites the laws of Kangho whenever he feels like it, setting up a clash of titans that will shake the underworld to its core.
