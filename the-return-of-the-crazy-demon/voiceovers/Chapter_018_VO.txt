Stepping out into the paved street beneath the clear afternoon sky, our boy Yi Zaha is suddenly ambushed by a terrifying martial expert brandishing a massive, six-foot nodachi that gleams like polished ice under the sun. This formidable warrior is So Gun-pyeong, a high-ranking vanguard captain from the Black Mystic Union whose devastating two-handed sword technique has slaughtered dozens of rival sect masters across the southern provinces. But looking closely at Zaha’s hands, you witness a level of unhinged disrespect that completely shatters every traditional rule of swordsmanship: rather than drawing a mastercrafted steel sword, our boy casually steps onto the cobblestones holding an everyday rectangular kitchen meat cleaver. In the eyes of ordinary martial artists, bringing a household cooking tool to a duel against a six-foot military nodachi is literal suicide. Yet from a tactical and kinetic standpoint, Zaha's internal calculations reveal the terrifying depth of a true grandmaster. He coldly analyzes the geometry of combat, recognizing that while So Gun-pyeong's greatsword possesses immense reach and destructive cutting momentum, every oversized weapon suffers from a fatal blind spot known as the inner dead zone. Once a swordsman commits to a full two-handed swing, the space directly against their chest is completely defenseless. Furthermore, infusing high-velocity internal qi into a brittle cleaver would simply shatter the kitchen tool on impact, making raw defense an inefficient waste of energy. Instead of relying on his weapon, Zaha relies on pure, microscopic body angling and spatial prediction. As the massive blade comes whistling down in a blinding flurry of arcs capable of slicing through stone pillars, Zaha effortlessly dances across the flagstones, evading each strike by fractions of a millimeter. Every time So Gun-pyeong attempts to accelerate his momentum, Zaha simply taps the flat of the heavy blade with his cooking knife, disrupting the swordsman's center of gravity and forcing him to stumble into awkward recoveries. Completely bewildered that an errand boy is parrying an elite nodachi with kitchenware, So Gun-pyeong leaps backward in a cold sweat, his breath ragged and his eyes wide with existential dread. Trembling in disbelief, the captain demands to know how a common waiter can predict every trajectory of a secret union sword style, hysterically asking if Zaha is secretly one of the legendary Twelve Heavenly Generals disguised as a servant. Zaha gives him a look of profound exhaustion, dryly pointing out how utterly ridiculous it is to scream at someone to shut up and then immediately demand to know who they are in the very next breath. Our boy bluntly explains that reading his movements isn't magic; So Gun-pyeong's footwork is so predictable and unrefined that any martial artist with functioning eyes could see his attacks coming from a mile away. Before the humiliated swordsman can readjust his grip, Zaha closes the distance in a ghost-like flash, slipping directly into the dead zone and delivering an explosive palm strike straight into the captain's chest, shutting down his breathing and knocking him unconscious across the cobblestones. Watching from the second-floor balcony of a neighboring tea house, an undercover martial artist from a prominent neutral clan clutches his teacup with trembling fingers, completely petrified as he realizes that the rumored mad errand boy of Ilyang Prefecture isn't an urban legend—he is an apex predator toying with the most dangerous killers in the province, casually treating high-stakes martial warfare like a quick break between morning chores. And with the syndicate's prized vanguard collapsed in the dirt, our boy prepares to drag his newest captive into the back rooms, ready to turn another dangerous enemy into an invaluable asset for the growing Low-Down Sect.
