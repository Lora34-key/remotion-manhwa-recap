The moment Wi Sun-woo and Gu Yang-su realize this is not some random back-alley scrub they can bully, their entire demeanor shifts from cocky predators to cornered wolves. Cha Sung-tae, meanwhile, is still on the muddy ground giving an Oscar-worthy performance of playing dead, secretly hoping both sides wipe each other out so he can loot the bodies and go back to running his shady side hustles. But Zaha sees right through the theatrical nonsense and immediately tells him to quit faking and stand up, which completely shatters the dramatic tension in the most hilarious way possible. What makes this fight so compelling from a tactical perspective is how Zaha manipulates their spatial positioning. Wi Sun-woo and Gu Yang-su rely on heavy ringed blades, weapons that require wide arcs, significant kinetic windup, and massive rotational clearance to generate lethal slicing power. In a cramped, cobblestone street soaked in torrential downpour, having two guys swinging giant butcher cleavers at the exact same target is actually an active liability because their strike zones constantly collide and restrict each other's footwork. Zaha understands this mechanical flaw instantly and deliberately moves between their blind spots. When Gu Yang-su clamps down on Zaha's whip and Wi Sun-woo rushes in to sever the cord with a burst of internal energy, they think they just neutralized his main tool. But looking over at the Dragon Head Smithy where Master Geum is watching from the window, Zaha dryly comments that so much for an unbreakable belief, casually tossing the ruined leather aside without a shred of panic. That is when Zaha draws the Black Dragon Sword, and the entire atmosphere turns suffocating. The moment internal qi surges through the steel, the blade begins glowing with a deep crimson radiance, sizzling furiously as cold raindrops vaporize on contact into a fine purple mist. Sung-tae is staring from the mud with his jaw practically glued to the pavement, completely awestruck, asking why the steel is glowing like an active volcanic forge. Zaha, being the unapologetic deadpan troll that he is, nonchalantly claims that this is exactly why his name is Zaha, which translates to purple sunset, turning what is essentially forbidden demonic cultivation into a completely made-up branding excuse right on the spot. In reality, Zaha is channeling the Fire Chicken Qi, the supreme thermal force of the Purple Cloud Divine Art, turning every swipe into superheated kinetic pressure waves. Instead of wasting precious internal reserves on condensed sword aura, Zaha unleashes wild blades of compressed heat wind directly into their faces, scorching their skin and blinding their vision while the torrential rain robs them of their balance. When Wi Sun-woo tries to force a desperate overhead execution stroke, Zaha slips beneath the guard with terrifying mechanical precision, driving his blade straight through the man's throat before reversing the momentum to sever Gu Yang-su's torso in one continuous red arc. The Twin Ghosts of the Ringed Blade collapse into the muddy crimson puddle, their terrifying underworld reputation wiped clean in less than two minutes. Cha Sung-tae is left trembling where he sits, realizing that trying to double-cross this man would be equivalent to speedrunning his own funeral. But Zaha does not even glance back at the bodies; he simply wipes the crimson rain from his face, looks at the ruined street, and calmly announces that it is time to go take a hot bath, treating a lethal gang war like an annoying chore between lunch and dinner. It proves that in the brutal Jianghu, the scariest martial artist is never the loudest brute, but the one who can dismantle elite killers with effortless chill and zero remorse.
