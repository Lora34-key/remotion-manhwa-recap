Just when you think Zaha can finally enjoy a peaceful, steam-filled soak after turning two elite killers into fertilizer, the Jianghu decides to remind him that rest does not exist for the wicked. While relaxing in the hot tub, listening to the heavy rain patter against the wooden roof, the entire hallway suddenly falls dead silent, an unnatural lack of background noise that immediately sets off his seasoned combat instincts. Instead of panicking, shouting for help, or scrambling for armor, Zaha just sits there completely naked in the steaming water, calmly waiting to see which suicidal clowns decided today was a good day to meet their ancestors. The door kicks open with a deafening crash, revealing Song U-geum and Yoo Jun-gu, the top enforcers from the rival brothel pavilions who foolishly figured catching the Mad Demon in his birthday suit was their ultimate golden ticket to glory. But attacking an unarmed martial artist means absolutely nothing when the man can turn an ordinary wooden bathtub into a lethal fragmentation grenade. Gathering the thermal pressure of the Fire Chicken Qi through his palms, Zaha rips a thick oak plank straight out of the tub frame, blasting an explosive shockwave of scalding water and jagged splinters directly into their faces. While both assassins are blinded, gasping, and reeling from the impact, Zaha charges forward completely bare-knuckled, swinging the heavy wooden board like a two-handed brutal war club. What follows is a scene of pure, unadulterated savagery that belongs in an underground horror movie. Song U-geum tries to counter with a desperate sword thrust, but Zaha kicks him in the solar plexus so hard his ribs implode, parries Yoo Jun-gu's wrist with the timber, and begins beating both men with ferocious, rhythmic overhead strikes. He is not just fighting to survive; he is deliberately using psychological terror to turn their bloodied remains into a permanent billboard for anyone else in Ilyang Prefecture who thinks about stepping out of line. By the time the wooden plank splinters into useless kindling, both enforcers are reduced to lifeless pulp on the bathhouse floor, their dark blood draining steadily into the sewer grates below. Still completely unclothed and dripping wet, Zaha picks up a discarded blade, steps out into the hallway, and casually asks a terrified maiden for a pair of pants like he just finished a light morning cardio workout. When he tracks down Cha Sung-tae, who was outside getting ambushed by their surviving subordinates, Zaha pins the cold tip of his sword right under Sung-tae's chin, demanding an immediate explanation. Sung-tae is sweating bullets, stammering that he told those idiots Zaha was a dangerous lunatic but they refused to listen to common sense. This interaction showcases Zaha's brilliant leadership psychology: instead of executing Sung-tae, he forces him to realize that viewing Zaha as just a simple errand boy is what got their comrades slaughtered in the first place. He orders Sung-tae to aggressively exaggerate his cruelty, to spread rumors that Zaha is an uncontrollable, flesh-tearing monster whose wrath guarantees instant death. In the criminal underworld, fear is the only currency that prevents unnecessary bloodshed; by establishing an aura of absolute madness, lesser thugs will surrender before a single punch is thrown. After confiscating Sung-tae's outer robes because he still needs a shirt, Zaha returns to the brothel, shuts down business for the day, and closes his eyes in quiet meditation, his chaotic journey proving that genuine peace in Jianghu is bought exclusively with iron and blood. It is the ultimate display of ruthless survival, demonstrating that when a true master throws hands, even the most mundane household furniture becomes an instrument of absolute destruction.
