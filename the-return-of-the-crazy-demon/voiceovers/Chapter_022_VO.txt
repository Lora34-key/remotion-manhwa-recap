With the dark blood still drying on the bathhouse floor and the broken corpses of Song U-geum and Yoo Jun-gu being hauled out like discarded garbage bags, Zaha steps out into the rain with Cha Sung-tae, casually wearing Sung-tae's confiscated silk coat because walking around half-naked is terrible for long-term sect branding. Sung-tae is having a full-blown existential crisis, desperately wondering how on earth a simple errand boy who used to wipe greasy soup off tavern tables became an unstoppable murder machine overnight, and more importantly, whether his own neck is next on the chopping block. But Zaha does not want to murder Sung-tae; what Zaha understands is that brute force can clear out rivals in the short term, but psychological dominance is what actually controls an entire criminal district. He turns to Sung-tae and gives him an absolute masterclass in underworld public relations: he explicitly orders him to blow Zaha's reputation completely out of proportion, to spread terrifying rumors to every street hustler, gang lieutenant, and merchant in Ilyang Prefecture that Zaha is an unhinged, merciless demon who eats internal qi for breakfast and rips people apart without blinking. In the brutal, dog-eat-dog ecosystem of the Unorthodox Faction, showing mercy is viewed as unforgivable weakness, but being perceived as an unpredictable lunatic with god-tier martial arts means people will obey out of sheer survival instinct before a single blade is drawn. Returning to the Plum Blossom Pavilion, Zaha surveys the empty tables and shuttered windows; the repeated violence has terrified normal patrons away, turning what used to be a buzzing entertainment hotspot into a desolate ghost town. Zaha orders business shut down for the day, not out of fear, but to let the district mourn its dead and reset its chaotic energy. That night, as Zaha sits in deep meditation inside Cho Yi-gyul's former quarters, the delicate, haunting sound of a woman singing drifts through the quiet wooden corridors. It is the senior courtesan Chae-hyang performing an ancient folk melody about a lonely woman applying red powder by the moonlit window, waiting for a wanderer who never comes home. The song captures the bittersweet, transient reality of the entertainment district, a fragile world where vulnerable people survive on hollow smiles and broken promises. Zaha listens in silence, his chaotic past life flooding back into his mind, reinforcing his internal resolve to change the tragic fate of this city. In his previous life, this entire prefecture was squeezed to death by greedy warlords and shattered by petty turf wars, leaving workers, courtesans, and laborers starving in the gutters. But this time around, Zaha is establishing the Low-Down Sect not to conquer the world like some generic megalomaniac, but to build an iron wall around the weak. Under his new regime, the brothels will no longer be extortion traps, workers will be fed, and every thug who tries to prey on the innocent will be brutally reminded why the red lanterns are hanging high once again. It is a moment of calm contemplation that bridges mindless violence with genuine purpose, proving that beneath Zaha's unhinged exterior lies a protector who has seen hell and refuses to let history repeat itself. By transforming fear into a protective shield, he ensures that the red lanterns will illuminate a safe haven rather than a slaughterhouse for the downtrodden. When an entire city learns to respect your boundaries, true peace begins. He knows the road ahead is drenched in blood, but every single step forward is carved with absolute conviction and purpose, showing that a true demon fights not for glory, but to protect those who cannot protect themselves.
