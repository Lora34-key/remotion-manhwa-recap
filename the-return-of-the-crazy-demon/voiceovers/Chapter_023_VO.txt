In the chaotic realm of the Jianghu, people usually assume that absolute power comes strictly from ancient martial manuals, divine heavenly elixirs, and legendary steel swords, but Zaha understands a fundamental economic truth that most bloodthirsty sect leaders completely overlook: wars destroy cities, and the people who actually rebuild civilization from the ashes are the architects. Every single time a major clash breaks out between Orthodox and Unorthodox factions, entire mountain fortresses, grand trading pavilions, and municipal quarters get leveled or burned to the ground, creating immense demand for elite craftsmen who understand structural engineering, masonry, and mechanical design. Enter Yeon Ja-seong, a sharp, sun-tanned young contractor who, in Zaha's previous life twenty years in the future, would rise to become one of the three greatest architectural masterminds in the entire martial world. Right now, though, Ja-seong is just a brilliant young professional recommended by Master Geum from the Dragon Head Smithy, arriving at the charred remnants of the Zaha Inn to bid on the comprehensive reconstruction project. What makes their initial encounter so thoroughly entertaining is Ja-seong's instant, shameless entrepreneurial hustle; the man does not show up as some timid laborer bowing in the mud, but immediately addresses Zaha as Leader, having heard through the grape vine that if he does an exceptional job, he might be appointed as the official permanent head of the newly minted Architectural Sect with zero monthly guild taxes. Zaha absolutely loves this level of bold, pragmatic confidence because Ja-seong is not a martial artist seeking pointless bloodshed; he is an ambitious builder who knows how to survive between warring factions through sharp communication, flawless craftsmanship, and mutual economic benefit. Ja-seong surveys the elevated hillside lot, pointing out the incredible natural ventilation and picturesque evening sunset views, offering a massive VIP discount under the clear condition that Zaha guarantees physical protection for the labor crew and provides hot evening meals on site. Zaha agrees without a second thought, immediately ordering Cha Sung-tae to mobilize staff from all three brothel pavilions every single evening to cook hot feasts for the entire construction squad. Sung-tae, feeling an instant wave of petty middle-management jealousy toward the newcomer, puffs out his chest and proudly declares himself the leader of the Rebirth Sect, claiming total operational authority over the entertainment district just so he does not look subordinate to a fresh young carpenter. Ja-seong hits him with the ultimate comedic reality check, asking with a bright smile if that still means Sung-tae is strictly beneath Leader Zaha anyway, which deflates Sung-tae's fragile ego right on the spot. Zaha even takes the captured arsonist thugs who originally torched his beloved inn and puts them to work under Sung-tae's watchful eye as hard manual laborers, turning criminal restitution into literal community rebuilding. When Ja-seong casually asks Zaha to treat him comfortably like a personal friend, Zaha immediately drops all formalities and addresses him simply as Ja-seong, prompting the flustered builder to reflexively call him Big Brother. This entire sequence highlights the true genius of Zaha's grand design: he is not just conquering territory through raw violence; he is assembling a complete society of blue-collar experts, blacksmiths, cooks, builders, and street-level operators into an unstoppable coalition under the banner of the Low-Down Sect. While arrogant noble clans view commoners as disposable trash, Zaha recognizes that a sect built on honest labor, hot food, and fair protection is far more durable than any empire founded purely on fear. By investing heavily in infrastructure, craftsmanship, and basic human dignity, he lays the unbreakable foundation for a completely new kind of underworld order that can withstand any martial storm.
