There is a cynical, unspoken rule in the martial world known as the Law of Equivalent Exchange: whenever something exceptionally fortunate happens to you, the universe immediately balances the cosmic ledger by throwing a fresh batch of absolute clowns directly into your path. Just moments after securing the brilliant young architect Yeon Ja-seong and making solid, inspiring plans to rebuild the Zaha Inn, Zaha finds his peaceful stroll interrupted by two messengers dressed in pristine matching silk uniforms, practically radiating corporate middle-management arrogance. The lead messenger, a smug operator named Hyeok Ryeon-hong, immediately blocks the cobblestone road and asks if he is speaking to Sect Leader Lee Zaha. Zaha, true to his unbothered deadpan persona, casually shakes his head and claims he is merely a simple inn errand boy who got lost on his way to fetch cooking groceries from the village butcher. Ryeon-hong sighs in deep annoyance, clearly tired of dealing with provincial backwater bumpkins, and announces that he represents the mighty Black Rabbit Union, delivering an exclusive summons from Ban Sa-woong, the master of the Golden Phoenix Pavilion. Now, from a tactical and narrative standpoint, this is where Zaha's regression knowledge becomes a terrifying advantage. Zaha knows exactly who Ban Sa-woong is without needing any briefing; in his past life, Sa-woong was a greedy, psychopathic money freak who climbed the ranks of the Unorthodox Faction through massive bribes and illegal extortion rackets, eventually running private contract killings until his own union discovered he was embezzling syndicate gold and chopped him into five pieces. Sa-woong's predatory taxes were the exact catalyst that forced lower-ranking gangsters to squeeze Ilyang Prefecture dry, creating a brutal domino effect that crushed common laborers, market vendors, and tavern keepers. To make their polite invitation even more charming, the second messenger unwraps a blood-soaked cloth bundle, revealing the freshly severed heads of Jeon Pung and Han Go-wook, the corrupt local monitors Zaha had previously forced to drink liquor instead of executing. Ryeon-hong smirks with insufferable condescension, explaining that the monitors were tortured and beheaded for covering up the prefecture's rebellion, but Sa-woong found the story of Zaha forcing them to drink so hilariously absurd that he decided to invite him for an audience instead of sending death squads right away. The messengers smugly advise Zaha that if he is terrified, he is welcome to pack his bags and flee the territory like a stray dog, claiming it would save both sides a lot of unnecessary bloodshed. For a split second, Zaha lets out a microscopic flicker of genuine killing intent, and the guard holding the severed heads instincts scream so violently that he grabs Ryeon-hong and leaps backward twenty paces in pure panic. It is a brilliant psychological beat because even when Zaha looks like an unarmed, scruffy youth, high-level martial killers can sense the terrifying aura of an apex predator lurking beneath his casual smirk. Zaha does not flinch at the severed heads or the veiled execution threat; instead, he simply smiles at the absurdity of a mid-tier bureaucrat telling the future Mad Demon to run away. Rather than fleeing, Zaha decides that if a corrupt syndicate billionaire wants to see him at his private mountain hunting villa, he might as well walk right through the front gates, crash the party, and inspect whatever expensive luxury items and gold bars are ripe for repossession. He accepts the invitation with a chilling, effortless grace, fully aware that when you invite an unhinged lunatic into your private estate, you are the one trapped inside with him. It sets the stage for a ruthless confrontation where wealth and status will meet the cold reality of absolute martial violence.
