When Zaha arrives at the secluded mountain hunting cabin, he is greeted by a sight that perfectly sums up the disgusting, bloated excess of the Unorthodox Faction: a roaring bonfire, long wooden tables overflowing with roasted game meat, and the Golden Phoenix Pavilion Master, Ban Sa-woong, lounging in heavy wild animal pelts like a provincial feudal warlord. Sa-woong looks considerably less like a refined martial artist and far more like an overgrown wild boar holding a hunting bow, which forces Zaha to suppress a genuine smirk the exact second he steps across the threshold. To immediately establish psychological dominance, Sa-woong has his armored guards slam the heavy iron doors shut with a deafening thud, trapping them inside, and arrogantly demands to know if this scruffy, unimpressive kid is truly the errand boy who dared to pocket his three lucrative brothel pavilions. Instead of bowing, trembling, or standing nervously at attention, Zaha casually grabs a heavy wooden chair, drags it right up to the roaring fire, and sits directly across from the boss with pure, unfiltered black-air-force energy. When Sa-woong furiously barks asking who gave him permission to sit and demands to know if he has completely lost his mind, Zaha looks him dead in the eyes and simply replies yes with a stone-cold, completely straight face. Realizing that empty verbal threats will not intimidate this kid, Sa-woong decides to pull a classic mob intimidation stunt: he signals his beast handler to bring out a massive, ravenous black hunting hound and orders Zaha to sit on the floor like a dog, commanding the vicious beast to tear his throat out when he refuses to budge. But this is where advanced martial physics meets absolute street-level disrespect. As the massive hound lunges forward with fangs bared, Zaha does not bother drawing a weapon or even flinching backward; he simply concentrates a microscopic, highly dense burst of Fire Chicken Qi into the tip of his index finger and flicks the charging dog directly on its wet nose. The kinetic shockwave sends the hundred-pound beast flying backward across the room, skidding ten feet into the dirt while whining in pure agony. The entire cabin goes dead silent. The armored guards are completely frozen, the dog trainer is having a visible existential crisis, and Zaha just rests his chin on his palms, mocks them by imitating a panting puppy, and calmly asks Sa-woong what he plans to offer in exchange for his own life. Zaha even starts performing an impromptu fortune-telling session, bluntly revealing that Sa-woong's facial structure proves he is destined to embezzle syndicate gold and get hacked into five bloody pieces by his own union masters. Hearing his deepest, most secretive criminal ambitions predicted so casually by a teenage kid breaks Sa-woong's composure into a thousand pieces. The boar-faced boss draws his heavy broadsword, sneering that he originally planned to keep Zaha as a useful pet but now has to put him down like vermin. But Zaha does not believe in letting generic villains finish their monologue. Before Sa-woong can even finish his sentence, Zaha's left boot snaps out in a thunderous kick that shatters the man's forearm guard, sending the obese pavilion master crashing violently into his own banquet table. It is the ultimate lesson in underworld diplomacy: never try to play psychological intimidation games with a lunatic who has already seen your future, lived through your demise, and considers your entire life's ambition nothing more than a cheap opening joke. When the dust settles and the room catches fire, only one man is left standing with zero regrets and absolute clarity.
