In the brutal hierarchy of the Unorthodox Faction, there is a fundamental psychological dynamic that separates true criminal empires from low-tier rabble: genuine criminal brotherhood. When an underground syndicate is genuinely bound by blood oaths and shared suffering, losing a comrade triggers an irrational, vengeful rage that can overwhelm even superior martial masters through sheer collective fury and reckless aggression. But as Zaha looks across the blood-splattered hunting cabin at the trembling henchmen kneeling on the floor, he knows instantly that these cowards possess none of that internal fire. Their bloated boss Ban Sa-woong is lying dead in his own pool of gore, and not a single one of these men is reaching for a sword; they are just desperately praying they do not end up on the evening slaughter menu. Instead of delivering some melodramatic hero monologue, Zaha pulls up a chair to the banquet table, grabs a piping-hot whole roasted chicken, and begins eating with absolute chill, treating the bloody massacre like a casual dinner show. When one of the guards nervously asks if they are going to be spared, Zaha chews calmly and warns them that he never beats dogs while eating, but if anyone dares interrupt his dinner with foolish heroic nonsense, he will turn into a rabid wolf and make their deaths infinitely more gruesome than their boss's. That is when a sharp-eyed man with a neat ponytail steps forward, introducing himself as Sima Bi, the pavilion's secret inspector. Sima Bi is clearly the only person in the room with more than two functioning brain cells; he admits honestly that he restrained the men from jumping in because he realized early on that Zaha was deliberately concealing his true martial realm behind the harmless facade of an inn errand boy. Zaha loves seeing someone who actually understands the art of hiding your true strength. Pointing a greasy chicken bone directly at him, Zaha declares that Sima Bi has the facial structure of a military strategist, which officially qualifies him to survive. But then Zaha drops the real tactical dilemma on them: if he lets them go, they will inevitably run back to the Black Rabbit Union, report the murder, and trigger an all-out syndicate war that will disrupt Zaha's peace. Sima Bi frantically promises they will never report it, but Zaha laughs in his face, hitting him with a devastating psychological reality check. Zaha explains that if Sima Bi returns to the union completely unharmed while his pavilion master is chopped to pieces, the union bosses will torture him to death for treason and utter incompetence, especially since Sima Bi was fully complicit in Sa-woong's illegal embezzlement schemes. Desperate to save his skin, Sima Bi pitches a brilliant criminal scheme: instead of reporting the death, all surviving henchmen will jointly loot the cabin's secret gold reserves, committing a mutual capital crime that legally binds them all together in permanent silence. Zaha smiles, thoroughly amused by how fast this corporate auditor transformed into an underworld conspirator. By forcing them to steal their own master's gold and pledging loyalty to his new order, Zaha flips an entire enemy garrison into compromised co-conspirators in less than ten minutes, proving once again that a master strategist conquers the mind long before conquering the body. In the treacherous politics of Jianghu, shared guilt is the strongest bond of all, turning enemies into obedient subordinates overnight. With gold in hand and their lives spared, they have officially joined the madman's unstoppable enterprise, learning that true leadership is about manipulating greed into absolute survival loyalty. Now, the real expansion begins.
