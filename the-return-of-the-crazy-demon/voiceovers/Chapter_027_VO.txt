When the heavy wooden wagons loaded with gold chests, silk bundles, and weapon crates begin rolling into the courtyard of the Plum Blossom Pavilion, Cha Sung-tae looks like he is about to suffer a genuine medical emergency. His face turns completely pale, his hands tremble violently, and he frantically rushes over to Zaha asking where on earth all this syndicate treasure came from, terrified that his boss has now graduated from street brawling to interstate grand larceny. Zaha, leaning casually against the wooden railing with an unbothered smirk, calmly informs him that this is simply the private retirement fund of the late Golden Phoenix Pavilion Master Ban Sa-woong, and that every single silver ingot will be reinvested directly into local infrastructure, worker salaries, and the grand reconstruction of the Zaha Inn. Sung-tae is utterly bewildered, asking how Zaha could possibly dare to loot the Black Rabbit Union, warning him that the union does not get robbed; they are the apex predators who track thieves down and beat the gold out of their severed bones. Zaha hits him with the most deadpan comedic logic possible, shrugging his shoulders and noting that since he is also exceptionally good at beating people up, everything should balance out just fine in the end. When Sung-tae groans in existential despair, asking what they will do if the entire Black Rabbit Union mobilizes a ruthless army to march on Ilyang Prefecture, Zaha casually suggests that he will simply beat up the Union Leader, tie him to a wooden pole, torture him a little bit, or force him to work as an errand boy in the new tavern. Sung-tae stares at him blankly, begging Zaha to at least say something sensible and realistic so the staff does not die of sheer panic. What Sung-tae does not know, of course, is that Zaha's regression memory gives him an absolute tactical overview: right now, the Black Rabbit Union Leader is locked away in deep seclusion trying to break through a martial bottleneck and cannot be bothered with provincial borders, meaning Zaha has a wide operational window to fortify his power base before the syndicate even realizes their southern branch has fallen. But recognizing that his followers need more than wild claims, Zaha calls a nighttime meeting by the campfire at the vacant inn lot, gathering Sung-tae, the young architect Yeon Ja-seong, and the blacksmith Master Geum. Looking at the crackling flames, Zaha lays out the true philosophical foundation of the Low-Down Sect. While arrogant noble clans and righteous Orthodox hypocrites treat everyday workers like disposable ants, Zaha is building a faction made entirely of the working class: cooks, carpenters, blacksmiths, courtesans, and laborers. He declares that in his territory, hard work will be respected, extortion will be punishable by death, and no outside bully will ever be allowed to step on their dignity again. The speech completely transforms the energy of his men; they stop seeing Zaha as just an unpredictable maniac and begin recognizing him as a genuine revolutionary leader who backs his unhinged humor with absolute protective power. Under the starry night, the fire illuminates a ragtag group of working-class misfits who realize they are no longer just surviving in the gutter, but laying the bricks for an empire that will shake the very foundations of the Jianghu. It turns chaotic ambition into a noble brotherhood that cannot be broken. With every coin counted and every promise sealed, the dawn of a new order rises over Ilyang Prefecture, proving that a true madman creates his own destiny while protecting his people with an iron fist.
