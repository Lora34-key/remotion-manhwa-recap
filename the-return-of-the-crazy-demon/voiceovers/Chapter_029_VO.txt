There is an unwritten, iron law in the Jianghu: the deadliest assassins rarely look like shadowy ninjas lurking on rooftops; they walk straight through the front door in broad daylight, order a steaming bowl of noodles, and politely compliment the chef before drawing their cold steel. While Zaha is relaxing at the Chunyang Restaurant, an intimidating, stoic warrior carrying an absurdly long nodachi broadsword calmly finishes his meal, places his copper coins on the wooden table, and courteously praises the kitchen cook before turning directly to Zaha. The swordsman introduces himself as So Gun-pyeong, an elite hitman dispatched by the Black Mystic Union, and casually asks the errand boy to step outside into the courtyard because the noodle shop is far too cramped for proper high-level swordplay. Jang Deuk-soo, trembling behind the kitchen counter while clutching a giant metal pot lid like a riot shield, whispers in absolute terror asking if Zaha can possibly survive against this towering monster, confessing that he accidentally insulted the swordsman earlier by asking if his weapon was forged by southern barbarians. Zaha dryly tells him to keep holding that pot lid for dear life and calmly steps out into the dirt alley. What makes So Gun-pyeong such a fascinating combatant is his weapon choice: he wields a colossal Japanese-style nodachi, a massive battlefield blade designed for devastating rotational momentum and long-range cleaving power that can bisect horse and armored rider in a single swing. In an open alley, getting caught within the rotational radius of that massive blade is instant death for ordinary fighters. But Zaha does not bother drawing the Black Dragon Sword; instead, he casually pulls out a heavy iron kitchen meat cleaver he grabbed from the restaurant prep table, treating an elite assassin duel like he is prepping pork ribs for the evening rush. Gun-pyeong is genuinely baffled by this level of absolute disrespect, but as he launches a blinding, two-handed diagonal strike meant to shear Zaha in half, Zaha's spatial positioning shines with masterclass precision. Instead of backing away into the sword's lethal strike arc, Zaha steps directly into the dead zone near the hilt, using the flat face of the heavy cleaver to deflect the kinetic momentum harmlessly into the cobblestones. Cha Sung-tae, who was hiding behind a rain barrel trying to gather the courage to intervene, gets accidentally clipped by the shockwave and collapses onto the ground, prompting Zaha to walk over and give Sung-tae two brisk wake-up slaps to snap him out of his panic. Zaha looks Gun-pyeong in the eye and begins analyzing his combat style out loud, dissecting the fundamental flaw in his footwork and pointing out that while the nodachi is terrifying at maximum extension, it leaves the wielder completely vulnerable in close-quarters infighting. Rather than slaughtering him, Zaha offers Gun-pyeong an official job as the chief martial arts instructor for the Low-Down Sect, noting that his disciplined demeanor and exceptional blade control make him the perfect man to train their ragtag recruits. Gun-pyeong is stunned, expecting a bloody death match but receiving a corporate recruitment pitch from an unhinged teenager holding a kitchen cleaver. This interaction showcases the core beauty of Zaha's character: he never kills talent when he can turn enemies into valuable comrades. By defusing the duel with effortless defense and psychological respect, Zaha secures another top-tier powerhouse for his faction, proving that true martial supremacy lies in bending hostile blades into protective shields. With an instructor secured, the foundation grows ever stronger, preparing the Low-Down Sect for the inevitable war with the regional syndicates, showing that brains and martial insight conquer raw ambition every time.
