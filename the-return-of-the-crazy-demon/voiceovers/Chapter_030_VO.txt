Watching two supreme martial artists clash in the middle of a dusty alleyway is supposed to look like an ethereal, cinematic wuxia ballet, but when Zaha is involved, high-level combat instantly devolves into a chaotic street brawl that leaves everyone questioning reality. The duel between So Gun-pyeong's massive nodachi and Zaha's garlic-chopping meat cleaver reaches its explosive climax when Gun-pyeong unleashes a tempest of grayish sword qi, slicing through the air like a localized hurricane. But Zaha does not even blink; channeling the vibrant crimson heat of the Fire Chicken Qi through the kitchen blade, he shreds the whirlwind apart with a wave of red energy that looks like boiling chili paste, smashing the flat face of the cleaver directly into Gun-pyeong's ribs. The kinetic shockwave sends the elite hitman flying through the air like an arrow, crashing into the cobblestones and rolling ten feet into the dirt. Jang Deuk-soo, watching from behind the restaurant door, drops his giant pot lid in pure shock, covering his ears and wondering if his neighborhood errand boy has secretly transformed into an ancient martial god. But what happens next is what truly defines Zaha's unhinged genius: instead of executing Gun-pyeong with a clean finishing strike, Zaha charges forward like an angry bull and begins pummeling him with bare-knuckle punches, turning a legendary duel of masters into a glorified bar fight. Punch, punch, punch, rhythmic and relentless, until Gun-pyeong is bruised, coughing blood, and completely immobilized by internal injuries. Deuk-soo is practically having a panic attack, frantically running over and begging Zaha to stop before he commits murder in broad daylight, worried that Zaha has succumbed to permanent madness. But Zaha just grabs Gun-pyeong by his hair, yanks his face up, and asks if he feels like surrendering yet. Gun-pyeong, despite spitting blood and having his pride dismantled, smirks with stubborn defiance, declaring that he would rather die than surrender to an errand boy. Zaha smiles with genuine admiration; in the treacherous Jianghu where cowards change masters like cheap robes, seeing a man hold onto his principles even while catching a bare-knuckle beating proves his true character. Zaha delivers one final, crisp slap across the cheek that knocks Gun-pyeong out cold, catching him before he hits the dirt and announcing that he likes this guy's stubborn spirit. When Deuk-soo asks how Zaha can like someone he just beat into a coma, Zaha explains that a man's true worth is only revealed after he gets his face beaten in and refuses to break. That is when Cha Sung-tae casually strolls into the alley, taking one look at the unconscious master sprawled on the ground and nonchalantly asking who Zaha is beating up in broad daylight this time, treating gang violence like a regular daily schedule. Zaha orders Gun-pyeong carried inside to be fed and patched up, preparing to turn this stubborn hitman into the backbone of his faction. It proves that Zaha does not just conquer rivals; he tests their soul under fire, forging unbreakable loyalty through kinetic diplomacy, sheer psychological dominance, and a deep understanding of human conviction that transforms former adversaries into lifelong brothers. In a cruel martial world driven purely by transactional betrayal, finding a warrior with real spine is rarer than finding dragon jade, and Zaha will happily crack a few ribs to recruit an honest man into his inner circle. With an elite swordsman brought into the fold, the Low-Down Sect is no longer just a loose collection of street thugs, but a rising syndicate capable of challenging the greatest powers in the underworld, and this is only the beginning of their bloody ascent.
