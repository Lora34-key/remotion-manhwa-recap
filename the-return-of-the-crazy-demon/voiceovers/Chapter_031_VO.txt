In the treacherous landscape of the Unorthodox Faction, capturing an elite assassin from a rival syndicate usually means one of two grim outcomes: brutal, limb-shattering torture or an immediate, unceremonious execution. So when So Gun-pyeong slowly regains consciousness inside the back room of the tavern, battered, bruised, and with his proud warrior spirit in absolute shambles, he fully expects to meet a grisly end. His face flushes crimson with humiliation as he swallows his pride and barely forces himself to mumble a desperate plea to be spared. Zaha, sitting across from him with a half-eaten snack in hand, immediately transforms the high-stakes life-or-death interrogation into an unhinged comedy routine. He asks Gun-pyeong if he has an elderly mother to support or a cute little daughter waiting at home, claiming that having a cute kid is an automatic VIP free pass in his newly formed sect, but since Gun-pyeong has neither, things are looking exceptionally grim for him. Gun-pyeong solemnly swears on his sword that if Zaha spares his life, he will never step foot in Ilyang Prefecture again and will never raise a weapon against anyone connected to the Low-Down Sect. This is where Zaha's strategic genius shines brightly through his apparent madness. Channeling the legendary Taoist strategist Zhuge Liang and his famous historical tactic of capturing and releasing a fierce barbarian king seven times to win true internal loyalty, Zaha decides to let Gun-pyeong walk away completely free. He knows that true warriors cannot be forced into lifelong obedience through raw physical fear alone; you have to let them experience your absolute superiority and undeniable magnanimity until their own warrior conscience compels them to bend the knee. Zaha looks him dead in the eyes and delivers an unforgettable warning: he is free to go and train to get stronger for a future rematch, but if Zaha ever hears a single rumor of Gun-pyeong bullying innocent merchants, peasants, or street workers, he will mobilize a million members of the Low-Down Sect to hunt him down and tear him limb from limb. When Gun-pyeong gasps in shock, asking if the Low-Down Sect truly has a million members, Zaha unbotheredly clarifies that every single merchant and laborer in the world belongs to his sect, whether they realize it or not, simply because Zaha decided so. Gun-pyeong bows with profound, genuine respect, swearing to honor his sacred oath until the day he dies before disappearing into the mist. As Gun-pyeong leaves, Cha Sung-tae pokes his head through the door, habitually shaking his nervous leg, asking why on earth Zaha let such a terrifying hitman go. Zaha mutters that lunatics should only fight lunatics, which prompts Sung-tae to insolently point his finger back and forth between Zaha and himself in agreement. Offended by the sheer disrespect, Zaha gathers crimson Fire Chicken Qi into his fingertip, demonstrating why pointing fingers in the Jianghu is a fatal habit by threatening to blast a hole through Sung-tae's skull with internal finger wind. Sung-tae throws his hands up in sheer panic, realizing that working for Zaha means living on a razor's edge between hilarious camaraderie and instant death. It is a brilliant sequence that establishes Zaha's long-term chess game: while rival syndicates burn bridges, Zaha is cultivating legendary debts of honor, proving that true kings rule by winning the respect of their deadliest enemies. By sparing a warrior of principle, he plants a seed that will bear fruit when the ultimate war for the underworld begins, proving that mercy backed by overwhelming strength is the greatest weapon of all, forging brotherhood out of hostility. As the road clears, his vision for the future stands tall and absolute.
