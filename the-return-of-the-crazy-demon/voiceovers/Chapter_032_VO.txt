The hilarious contrast between high-stakes martial warfare and everyday provincial life in Ilyang Prefecture is what makes Zaha such an unforgettable protagonist. Right after sparing an elite hitman and threatening his own lieutenant with finger-wind execution, what does Zaha do? He walks straight back into the Chunyang Restaurant with pork grease on his shirt because the man has not eaten lunch yet. Sitting at a rough wooden table with Jang Deuk-soo and So Gun-pyeong, the three of them dig into a mountain of braised pork ribs, their fingers coated in glistening pork fat. Gun-pyeong, who literally tried to slice Zaha in half an hour ago, is now sitting awkwardly across from him, nibbling on tender ribs while sweating bullets. When Zaha asks if the ribs are delicious, Gun-pyeong politely says yes, only for Zaha to roast him on the spot, questioning why an enemy hitman who refused to join the Low-Down Sect is getting VIP regular-customer privileges. Deuk-soo nags Zaha to let the man eat in peace, creating this absurd, family-style domestic comedy in the middle of a brutal gang war. That is when Zaha notices Gun-pyeong's prized weapon, the Night Blade, resting quietly on the table. In the Unorthodox Faction, this nodachi is practically legendary, forged from rare southern steel designed to channel intense sword wind. Gun-pyeong treats handing it over like parting with his own soul, terrified that Zaha will snap it in half out of spite. Zaha casually wipes the grease off his fingers on his trousers, draws the cold steel from its scabbard, and flicks the blade with a microscopic touch of internal qi. The steel rings with a deep, resonant hum that confirms its remarkable metallurgical balance and craftsmanship. Zaha admits it is a genuine masterwork, but then hits him with the ultimate comedic disrespect: he laughs out loud and points out that as legendary as this treasured sword is, it still got its ass handed to it by a standard household meat cleaver used for dicing garlic and chopping spring onions. Deuk-soo bursts into roaring laughter alongside Zaha, but when he accidentally meets Gun-pyeong's cold assassin stare, the poor tavern keeper freezes mid-laugh and ducks behind his soup pot. Zaha instantly glares at Gun-pyeong, demanding to know who gave him permission to give death glares to his favorite noodle chef, forcing the terrifying swordsman to apologize and look at the cook with polite customer manners. This scene provides profound insight into Zaha's martial philosophy regarding weapons and men. In the Jianghu, possessing a blade far superior to your martial realm makes you a walking target for greedy masters, while carrying a weapon inferior to your skills leads to untimely death when high-tier weapons clash. A weapon is only as good as the internal conviction and biological precision of the hand wielding it. But as they finish the meal, grim intelligence arrives from the northern border: the Black Rabbit Union has officially mobilized their vanguard forces to retaliate for the loss of their gold and pavilions. The peace of Ilyang Prefecture has expired, and Zaha decides that instead of waiting behind wooden barricades for the enemy to surround them, he is going to take the initiative, grab a few executioner axes, and bring the war straight to the syndicate's front door. He gathers his men, checks his edge, and steps out into the gathering darkness ready to remind the underworld why playing defense is for cowards and offense is the only true shield. With laughter fading and battle lines drawn, the hunt begins across the rugged borders, paving the way for the ultimate showdown.
