Most sane martial artists planning to attack a rival syndicate headquarters spend weeks gathering intelligence, mapping out escape routes, and organizing stealth night raids. But Zaha does not operate on normal human logic; he grabs a colossal blacksmith's executioner broadaxe, slaps it casually over his shoulder, and strolls right up to the massive fortified gates of the Black Rabbit Union in broad daylight. So Gun-pyeong, who followed along to witness the madness firsthand, suggests they breach the walls together, but Zaha tells him to hang back and watch the masterclass unfold. Zaha knocks loudly on the heavy iron-studded gate, and when the gatekeeper peeks through the peephole asking who is knocking, Zaha introduces himself as Lee Zaha from Ilyang Prefecture and tells him to open up before he splinters the timber into toothpicks. When the guard laughs and starts shouting inside about an arrogant pale-faced kid making empty threats, Zaha loses patience, leaps twenty feet straight onto the rooftop ridge, and surveys the luxurious scholar-style estate. Seeing how comfortably these extortionist parasites are living off stolen blood money genuinely annoys him. As dozens of heavily armed enforcers rush into the courtyard hurling foul curses and drawing their sabers, Zaha drops down into the center of the pavilion with a deafening thud. In the ultimate display of psychological dominance, Zaha calmly drags a wooden chair into the middle of the cobblestone courtyard, jams the massive executioner axe head-down into the stone floor, and sits down with his legs crossed like a bored emperor waiting for court to convene. The guards are completely disoriented; nobody rushes in because the sheer aura of unbothered lunacy makes them suspect an invisible army is waiting in the wings. High-ranking syndicate executives flood the courtyard, forming a tight perimeter, followed by an elderly elder who asks if Zaha came alone. Zaha scoffs, telling the old fossil that he is not here to chat with decrepit minions and loudly demands the rabbit-masked boss to step outside. That is when the supreme leader of the Black Rabbit Union emerges, wearing his signature black rabbit mask. Looking down from the grand balcony, the leader asks with disgust if this scruffy teenager is the errand boy causing trouble. Zaha does not just respond; he unleashes the most devastating psychological verbal assault in Jianghu history. Answering a question with a question, Zaha asks if he is the personal slave rabbit of the regional overlord Dae Na-chal, openly mocking him as a cowardly rodent hiding behind painted leather who plays warlord in a rural prefecture while bowing like a beaten dog to higher powers. Zaha then addresses the entire syndicate out loud, proclaiming that if their leader has the warrior honor to fight him one-on-one, he deserves to lead them, but if he hides behind his subordinates like a trembling coward, he is nothing more than an incompetent parasite and a fraud. Gun-pyeong, listening from the perimeter wall, shakes his head in utter disbelief as the entire compound freezes in stunned silence. By stripping the syndicate boss of his dignity, psychological authority, and institutional credibility in front of his entire garrison, Zaha forces a life-or-death duel on his own terms, setting up the most brutal leadership usurpation the criminal underworld has ever witnessed. When pride is backed against a wall, only blood can settle the score, and Zaha is more than ready to show this entire city what real martial power looks like. In a world ruled by raw survival, the man who controls the room without throwing a single punch is the true apex predator, and the slaughter is about to begin.
