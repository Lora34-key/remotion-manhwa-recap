Zaha looks Gun-pyeong in the eye and begins analyzing his combat style out loud, dissecting the fundamental flaw in his footwork and pointing out that while the nodachi is terrifying at maximum extension, it leaves the wielder completely vulnerable in close-quarters infighting. Rather than slaughtering him, Zaha offers Gun-pyeong an official job as the chief martial arts instructor for the Low-Down Sect, noting that his disciplined demeanor and exceptional blade control make him the perfect man to train their ragtag recruits. Gun-pyeong is stunned, expecting a bloody death match but receiving a corporate recruitment pitch from an unhinged teenager holding a kitchen cleaver. This interaction showcases the core beauty of Zaha's character: he never kills talent when he can turn enemies into valuable comrades. By defusing the duel with effortless defense and psychological respect, Zaha secures another top-tier powerhouse for his faction, proving that true martial supremacy lies in bending hostile blades into protective shields. With an instructor secured, the foundation grows ever stronger, preparing the Low-Down Sect for the inevitable war with the regional syndicates, showing that brains and martial insight conquer raw ambition every time.

Watching two supreme martial artists clash in the middle of a dusty alleyway is supposed to look like an ethereal, cinematic wuxia ballet, but when Zaha is involved, high-level combat instantly devolves into a chaotic street brawl that leaves everyone questioning reality. The duel between So Gun-pyeong's massive nodachi and Zaha's garlic-chopping meat cleaver reaches its explosive climax when Gun-pyeong unleashes a tempest of grayish sword qi, slicing through the air like a localized hurricane. But Zaha does not even blink; channeling the vibrant crimson heat of the Fire Chicken Qi through the kitchen blade, he shreds the whirlwind apart with a wave of red energy that looks like boiling chili paste, smashing the flat face of the cleaver directly into Gun-pyeong's ribs. The kinetic shockwave sends the elite hitman flying through the air like an arrow, crashing into the cobblestones and rolling ten feet into the dirt. Jang Deuk-soo, watching from behind the restaurant door, drops his giant pot lid in pure shock, covering his ears and wondering if his neighborhood errand boy has secretly transformed into an ancient martial god. But what happens next is what truly defines Zaha's unhinged genius: instead of executing Gun-pyeong with a clean finishing strike, Zaha charges forward like an angry bull and begins pummeling him with bare-knuckle punches, turning a legendary duel of masters into a glorified bar fight. Punch, punch, punch, rhythmic and relentless, until Gun-pyeong is bruised, coughing blood, and completely immobilized by internal injuries. Deuk-soo is practically having a panic attack, frantically running over and begging Zaha to stop before he commits murder in broad daylight, worried that Zaha has succumbed to permanent madness. But Zaha just grabs Gun-pyeong by his hair, yanks his face up, and asks if he feels like surrendering yet. Gun-pyeong, despite spitting blood and having his pride dismantled, smirks with stubborn defiance, declaring that he would rather die than surrender to an errand boy. Zaha smiles with genuine admiration; in the treacherous Jianghu where cowards change masters like cheap robes, seeing a man hold onto his principles even while catching a bare-knuckle beating proves his true character. Zaha delivers one final, crisp slap across the cheek that knocks Gun-pyeong out cold, catching him before he hits the dirt and announcing that he likes this guy's stubborn spirit. When Deuk-soo asks how Zaha can like someone he just beat into a coma, Zaha explains that a man's true worth is only revealed after he gets his face beaten in and refuses to break. That is when Cha Sung-tae casually strolls into the alley, taking one look at the unconscious master sprawled on the ground and nonchalantly asking who Zaha is beating up in broad daylight this time, treating gang violence like a regular daily schedule. Zaha orders Gun-pyeong carried inside to be fed and patched up, preparing to turn this stubborn hitman into the backbone of his faction. It proves that Zaha does not just conquer rivals; he tests their soul under fire, forging unbreakable loyalty through kinetic diplomacy, sheer psychological dominance, and a deep understanding of human conviction that transforms former adversaries into lifelong brothers. In a cruel martial world driven purely by transactional betrayal, finding a warrior with real spine is rarer than finding dragon jade, and Zaha will happily crack a few ribs to recruit an honest man into his inner circle. With an elite swordsman brought into the fold, the Low-Down Sect is no longer just a loose collection of street thugs, but a rising syndicate capable of challenging the greatest powers in the underworld, and this is only the beginning of their bloody ascent.

In the treacherous landscape of the Unorthodox Faction, capturing an elite assassin from a rival syndicate usually means one of two grim outcomes: brutal, limb-shattering torture or an immediate, unceremonious execution. So when So Gun-pyeong slowly regains consciousness inside the back room of the tavern, battered, bruised, and with his proud warrior spirit in absolute shambles, he fully expects to meet a grisly end. His face flushes crimson with humiliation as he swallows his pride and barely forces himself to mumble a desperate plea to be spared. Zaha, sitting across from him with a half-eaten snack in hand, immediately transforms the high-stakes life-or-death interrogation into an unhinged comedy routine. He asks Gun-pyeong if he has an elderly mother to support or a cute little daughter waiting at home, claiming that having a cute kid is an automatic VIP free pass in his newly formed sect, but since Gun-pyeong has neither, things are looking exceptionally grim for him. Gun-pyeong solemnly swears on his sword that if Zaha spares his life, he will never step foot in Ilyang Prefecture again and will never raise a weapon against anyone connected to the Low-Down Sect. This is where Zaha's strategic genius shines brightly through his apparent madness. Channeling the legendary Taoist strategist Zhuge Liang and his famous historical tactic of capturing and releasing a fierce barbarian king seven times to win true internal loyalty, Zaha decides to let Gun-pyeong walk away completely free. He knows that true warriors cannot be forced into lifelong obedience through raw physical fear alone; you have to let them experience your absolute superiority and undeniable magnanimity until their own warrior conscience compels them to bend the knee. Zaha looks him dead in the eyes and delivers an unforgettable warning: he is free to go and train to get stronger for a future rematch, but if Zaha ever hears a single rumor of Gun-pyeong bullying innocent merchants, peasants, or street workers, he will mobilize a million members of the Low-Down Sect to hunt him down and tear him limb from limb. When Gun-pyeong gasps in shock, asking if the Low-Down Sect truly has a million members, Zaha unbotheredly clarifies that every single merchant and laborer in the world belongs to his sect, whether they realize it or not, simply because Zaha decided so. Gun-pyeong bows with profound, genuine respect, swearing to honor his sacred oath until the day he dies before disappearing into the mist. As Gun-pyeong leaves, Cha Sung-tae pokes his head through the door, habitually shaking his nervous leg, asking why on earth Zaha let such a terrifying hitman go. Zaha mutters that lunatics should only fight lunatics, which prompts Sung-tae to insolently point his finger back and forth between Zaha and himself in agreement. Offended by the sheer disrespect, Zaha gathers crimson Fire Chicken Qi into his fingertip, demonstrating why pointing fingers in the Jianghu is a fatal habit by threatening to blast a hole through Sung-tae's skull with internal finger wind. Sung-tae throws his hands up in sheer panic, realizing that working for Zaha means living on a razor's edge between hilarious camaraderie and instant death. It is a brilliant sequence that establishes Zaha's long-term chess game: while rival syndicates burn bridges, Zaha is cultivating legendary debts of honor, proving that true kings rule by winning the respect of their deadliest enemies. By sparing a warrior of principle, he plants a seed that will bear fruit when the ultimate war for the underworld begins, proving that mercy backed by overwhelming strength is the greatest weapon of all, forging brotherhood out of hostility. As the road clears, his vision for the future stands tall and absolute.

The hilarious contrast between high-stakes martial warfare and everyday provincial life in Ilyang Prefecture is what makes Zaha such an unforgettable protagonist. Right after sparing an elite hitman and threatening his own lieutenant with finger-wind execution, what does Zaha do? He walks straight back into the Chunyang Restaurant with pork grease on his shirt because the man has not eaten lunch yet. Sitting at a rough wooden table with Jang Deuk-soo and So Gun-pyeong, the three of them dig into a mountain of braised pork ribs, their fingers coated in glistening pork fat. Gun-pyeong, who literally tried to slice Zaha in half an hour ago, is now sitting awkwardly across from him, nibbling on tender ribs while sweating bullets. When Zaha asks if the ribs are delicious, Gun-pyeong politely says yes, only for Zaha to roast him on the spot, questioning why an enemy hitman who refused to join the Low-Down Sect is getting VIP regular-customer privileges. Deuk-soo nags Zaha to let the man eat in peace, creating this absurd, family-style domestic comedy in the middle of a brutal gang war. That is when Zaha notices Gun-pyeong's prized weapon, the Night Blade, resting quietly on the table. In the Unorthodox Faction, this nodachi is practically legendary, forged from rare southern steel designed to channel intense sword wind. Gun-pyeong treats handing it over like parting with his own soul, terrified that Zaha will snap it in half out of spite. Zaha casually wipes the grease off his fingers on his trousers, draws the cold steel from its scabbard, and flicks the blade with a microscopic touch of internal qi. The steel rings with a deep, resonant hum that confirms its remarkable metallurgical balance and craftsmanship. Zaha admits it is a genuine masterwork, but then hits him with the ultimate comedic disrespect: he laughs out loud and points out that as legendary as this treasured sword is, it still got its ass handed to it by a standard household meat cleaver used for dicing garlic and chopping spring onions. Deuk-soo bursts into roaring laughter alongside Zaha, but when he accidentally meets Gun-pyeong's cold assassin stare, the poor tavern keeper freezes mid-laugh and ducks behind his soup pot. Zaha instantly glares at Gun-pyeong, demanding to know who gave him permission to give death glares to his favorite noodle chef, forcing the terrifying swordsman to apologize and look at the cook with polite customer manners. This scene provides profound insight into Zaha's martial philosophy regarding weapons and men. In the Jianghu, possessing a blade far superior to your martial realm makes you a walking target for greedy masters, while carrying a weapon inferior to your skills leads to untimely death when high-tier weapons clash. The peace of Ilyang Prefecture has expired, and Zaha decides that instead of waiting behind wooden barricades for the enemy to surround them, he is going to take the initiative, grab a few executioner axes, and bring the war straight to the syndicate's front door. He gathers his men, checks his edge, and steps out into the gathering darkness ready to remind the underworld why playing defense is for cowards and offense is the only true shield. With laughter fading and battle lines drawn, the hunt begins across the rugged borders, paving the way for the ultimate showdown.

Most sane martial artists planning to attack a rival syndicate headquarters spend weeks gathering intelligence, mapping out escape routes, and organizing stealth night raids. But Zaha does not operate on normal human logic; he grabs a colossal blacksmith's executioner broadaxe, slaps it casually over his shoulder, and strolls right up to the massive fortified gates of the Black Rabbit Union in broad daylight. So Gun-pyeong, who followed along to witness the madness firsthand, suggests they breach the walls together, but Zaha tells him to hang back and watch the masterclass unfold. Zaha knocks loudly on the heavy iron-studded gate, and when the gatekeeper peeks through the peephole asking who is knocking, Zaha introduces himself as Lee Zaha from Ilyang Prefecture and tells him to open up before he splinters the timber into toothpicks. When the guard laughs and starts shouting inside about an arrogant pale-faced kid making empty threats, Zaha loses patience, leaps twenty feet straight onto the rooftop ridge, and surveys the luxurious scholar-style estate. Seeing how comfortably these extortionist parasites are living off stolen blood money genuinely annoys him.
